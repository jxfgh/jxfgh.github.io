Research

My research examines how graph-based machine learning can help detect and interpret fraud in financial systems. My doctoral work focused on smart-contract fraud detection. I am interested in connecting these technical methods with financial analytics, regulatory practice, and decision-making.

Research directions
  • Multi-view graph learning for smart-contract fraud detection
  • Self-supervised detection when labelled examples are limited
  • Temporal graph models for evolving transaction networks
  • Explainable graph reasoning for investigation and oversight
Teaching interests

Financial technology and digital finance; AI, machine learning and analytics in finance; blockchain and smart contracts; digital currencies and CBDCs; fraud, risk analytics, and RegTech.

My teaching connects technical foundations with the financial institutions and regulatory settings in which these tools are applied.
